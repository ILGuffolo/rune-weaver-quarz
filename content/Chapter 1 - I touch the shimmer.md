The woods behind Riverside High were not particularly impressive.

At least, that was Connor's opinion.

"They're barely even woods," he complained, stepping over a fallen branch. "It's like a... i don't know, a very overgrown bush."

Mila laughed.

"You're just upset because we haven't found anything."

"We found a squirrel."

"It wasn't a very suspicious squirrel."

"The squirrel was eating a pinecone."

"Exactly."

Connor groaned while Mila grinned at him. A few steps behind, their friend Jenna shook her head. "We're not supposed to be back here."

Mila didn't slow down. Her sneakers crunched through dead leaves

"Mila, seriously." Jenna jogged to catch up, blonde ponytail bobbing in sync with her worried expression. "If we get caught—"

"We won't." Mila ducked under a low branch, the afternoon sun filtered through the leaves, painting shifting patterns across the trail. 

There was something nice about being here.

No classes.

No homework.

No expectations.

Just... Freedom.

Mila vaulted over a fallen log.

"Hey."

Connor nearly walked into her.

"What now?"

She pointed.

"That."

It sat between two old pines like a trick of the light. A shimmer, faint and vertical, maybe two meters tall — like asphalt on hot summer days, except the air was cold, and the distortion was staying perfectly still.

Nobody spoke.

Finally Connor broke the silence.

"Okay."

A pause.

"What the hell is that?"

it reminded Mila of a torn piece of paper. but it was reality itself being torn, and twisted.

"We _really_ shouldn't be here" Jenna whispered.

But she was already moving toward it.

"Mila," Jenna warned.

"I'm just looking."

"You say that every time." Connor snorted.

Mila scoffed

The closer she got, the stranger the air felt.

Cool.

Electric.

"Maybe don't touch the obvious magic thing," Connor suggested.

She reached out with the stick she'd been carrying since the fence — pure habit, always have a poking stick, basic survival strategy — and touched the edge of the distortion.

The stick went through.

"Okay," said Connor. "Okay, absolutely not—"

She leaned closer. The shimmer pulled at the end of the stick, gently, like a current of water. She could feel a faint warmth against her face.

"don't freak out" she said to her friends without even turning.

She lifted a hand.

"Maybe if I just—"

The portal exploded with light.

"Mila!"

Connor's voice sounded distant.

The world twisted.

A violent gust of wind slammed into her, the ground swapping places with the sky and the sky swapping places with everything else, all in the same instant, and then—

For one terrifying second there was nothing at all.

Then the ground hit her

"Ow."

Silence answered her.

she pushed herself upright.

The first thing she noticed was the color.

It was everywhere — in the bark of the trees, which was too dark, a deep reddish-brown like dried blood; in the grass, which shouldn't have been pink but was, a dull and dusty rose that covered the ground as far as she could see; in the dirt itself, which was the color of rust. Even the light felt different, coming through the canopy at the wrong angle, filtered through leaves that were too large and a shade too dark.

Mila stood very still.

"Jenna," she whispered.

The forest gave her nothing back.

"Connor."

"Guys!" she screamed

Silence. 

Only wind moving through unfamiliar trees.

She turned around. There was no shimmer. No gap between two familiar pines. Just more trees, their reddish trunks stretching away in every direction, identical and impossible.

_Okay_, she thought. _Okay. This is fine. This is completely fine._

She made a fist to stop her hands from shaking.

"Okay," she muttered.

Her voice shook.

"Don't freak out."

She immediately freaked out.

The next few hours were miserable.

She looked for the portal for two hours before she admitted to herself she had no idea where it had been. The trees all looked the same. The pink grass didn't take footprints the way regular grass did.

She wandered through the forest.

Got lost.

Tried to retrace her steps.

Got even more lost.

Eventually she forced herself to stop.

Panicking wasn't helping.

She sat beneath a massive tree and took several deep breaths.

Think.

Food.

Water.

Shelter.

One problem at a time.

By sunset she had found a stream and built what could generously be described as a shelter.

It leaned.

Badly.

Several sticks appeared to be supporting absolutely nothing.

Still.

It existed.

That counted.

As darkness settled over the forest, tiny glowing creatures emerged from the trees.

Blue lights drifted through the night air.

Beautiful.

Peaceful.

Alien.

Mila sat beside the stream and hugged her knees.

For the first time all day there was nobody around.

Nobody to annoy her.

Nobody to argue with.

Nobody to laugh at her jokes.

The loneliness hit harder than she expected.

She thought about home.

About her parents.

About Connor's endless nonsense.

About Jenna pretending not to care.

A lump formed in her throat.

Mila hated crying.

She was sixteen years old and she had survived middle school and two years of her parents' divorce and that horrible six months when she and Jenna weren't speaking, and she was not going to fall apart in a magical forest because of a stupid shimmer-thingy.

She wiped her eyes.

"This is insane"

Her voice sounded small.

But determined.

"I'm getting home."

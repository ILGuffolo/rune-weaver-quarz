# Chapter 1 - I touch the shimmer

The woods behind Riverside High were not particularly impressive.

At least, that was Connor's opinion.

"They're barely even woods," he complained, stepping over a fallen branch. "It's like a... i don't know, a very overgrown bush."

Mila laughed.

"You're just upset because we haven't found anything."

"We found a squirrel."

"It wasn't a very suspicious squirrel."

"The squirrel was eating a pinecone."

"Exactly."

Connor groaned while Mila grinned at him. A few steps behind, their friend Jenna shook her head. "We're not supposed to be back here."

Mila didn't slow down. Her sneakers crunched through dead leaves

"Mila, seriously." Jenna jogged to catch up, blonde ponytail bobbing in sync with her worried expression. "If we get caught—"

"We won't." Mila ducked under a low branch, the afternoon sun filtered through the leaves, painting shifting patterns across the trail. 

There was something nice about being here.

No classes.

No homework.

No expectations.

Just... Freedom.

Mila vaulted over a fallen log.

"Hey."

Connor nearly walked into her.

"What now?"

She pointed.

"That."

It sat between two old pines like a trick of the light. A shimmer, faint and vertical, maybe two meters tall — like asphalt on hot summer days, except the air was cold, and the distortion was staying perfectly still.

Nobody spoke.

Finally Connor broke the silence.

"Okay."

A pause.

"What the hell is that?"

it reminded Mila of a torn piece of paper. but it was reality itself being torn, and twisted.

"We _really_ shouldn't be here" Jenna whispered.

But she was already moving toward it.

"Mila," Jenna warned.

"I'm just looking."

"You say that every time." Connor snorted.

Mila scoffed

The closer she got, the stranger the air felt.

Cool.

Electric.

"Maybe don't touch the obvious magic thing," Connor suggested.

She reached out with the stick she'd been carrying since the fence — pure habit, always have a poking stick, basic survival strategy — and touched the edge of the distortion.

The stick went through.

"Okay," said Connor. "Okay, absolutely not—"

She leaned closer. The shimmer pulled at the end of the stick, gently, like a current of water. She could feel a faint warmth against her face.

"don't freak out" she said to her friends without even turning.

She lifted a hand.

"Maybe if I just—"

The portal exploded with light.

"Mila!"

Connor's voice sounded distant.

The world twisted.

A violent gust of wind slammed into her, the ground swapping places with the sky and the sky swapping places with everything else, all in the same instant, and then—

For one terrifying second there was nothing at all.

Then the ground hit her

"Ow."

Silence answered her.

she pushed herself upright.

The first thing she noticed was the color.

It was everywhere — in the bark of the trees, which was too dark, a deep reddish-brown like dried blood; in the grass, which shouldn't have been pink but was, a dull and dusty rose that covered the ground as far as she could see; in the dirt itself, which was the color of rust. Even the light felt different, coming through the canopy at the wrong angle, filtered through leaves that were too large and a shade too dark.

Mila stood very still.

"Jenna," she whispered.

The forest gave her nothing back.

"Connor."

"Guys!" she screamed

Silence. 

Only wind moving through unfamiliar trees.

She turned around. There was no shimmer. No gap between two familiar pines. Just more trees, their reddish trunks stretching away in every direction, identical and impossible.

_Okay_, she thought. _Okay. This is fine. This is completely fine._

She made a fist to stop her hands from shaking.

"Okay," she muttered.

Her voice shook.

"Don't freak out."

She immediately freaked out.

The next few hours were miserable.

She looked for the portal for two hours before she admitted to herself she had no idea where it had been. The trees all looked the same. The pink grass didn't take footprints the way regular grass did.

She wandered through the forest.

Got lost.

Tried to retrace her steps.

Got even more lost.

Eventually she forced herself to stop.

Panicking wasn't helping.

She sat beneath a massive tree and took several deep breaths.

Think.

Food.

Water.

Shelter.

One problem at a time.

By sunset she had found a stream and built what could generously be described as a shelter.

It leaned.

Badly.

Several sticks appeared to be supporting absolutely nothing.

Still.

It existed.

That counted.

As darkness settled over the forest, tiny glowing creatures emerged from the trees.

Blue lights drifted through the night air.

Beautiful.

Peaceful.

Alien.

Mila sat beside the stream and hugged her knees.

For the first time all day there was nobody around.

Nobody to annoy her.

Nobody to argue with.

Nobody to laugh at her jokes.

The loneliness hit harder than she expected.

She thought about home.

About her parents.

About Connor's endless nonsense.

About Jenna pretending not to care.

A lump formed in her throat.

Mila hated crying.

She was sixteen years old and she had survived middle school and two years of her parents' divorce and that horrible six months when she and Jenna weren't speaking, and she was not going to fall apart in a magical forest because of a stupid shimmer-thingy.

She wiped her eyes.

"This is insane"

Her voice sounded small.

But determined.

"I'm getting home."

# Chapter 2 - I almost die, Twice

She made it through the night; She would neve admit it, but she felt proud.

The next morning brought better luck.

She found a stream eventually, because she was listening for it, and because the basic logic of _water flows somewhere_ was the one piece of survival knowledge she had retained from that camping trip in seventh grade where she'd spent most of the weekend chatting.

She drank. The water tasted normal. "Finally, something that makes sense".

she laughed at herself. then realized she was alone, and stopped.

The animals sounded normal too, mostly, except once she heard something move through the undergrowth to her east that sounded considerably larger than anything she would have liked, and she climbed the nearest tree and stayed there for half an hour until whatever it was moved on.

By mid-morning the next day she had found more water, eaten some berries that she was about seventy percent sure weren't poisonous , and walked in what she hoped was a consistent direction. She'd pulled bark off a tree to try to leave a trail marker. She felt like she was starring in a survival documentary where the crew had forgotten to show up.

The animal that found her was roughly the size of a very large dog and had too many teeth for anything she would call fair.

She didn't see it until it was between her and the stream, head down, tracking something in the pink grass. She stopped. It raised its head and looked at her with small, pale eyes.

She took one careful step backward.

It took two steps toward her.

She bolted. This was, she would later reflect, probably not the optimal survival strategy, but it was the one she had, and she committed to it fully, sprinting through the reddish underbrush with the animal behind her, crashing through undergrowth that tore at her jeans, lungs already burning—

She heard something flying through the air, then a thud, and then silence.

She ran three more steps before her brain caught up and she stumbled to a halt, grabbing a tree trunk for balance, and looked back.

The animal was on the ground. An arrow protruded from its flank

Two figures emerged from the trees.

Both carried bows.

Both wore green cloaks patterned to blend into the forest.

Hunters.

The older one spotted her immediately.

Both stopped.

Mila stopped.

Nobody moved.

Several very awkward seconds passed.

Then the younger hunter slowly lowered his bow.

The older hunter said something she couldn't understand.

Mila blinked.

"Uh."

She pointed at herself.

"Lost."

Then gestured wildly at the forest.

"Very lost."

The hunters exchanged glances.

The younger one laughed.

Apparently that translated perfectly.

A few minutes later they offered her food.

Mila accepted immediately.

The hunters introduced themselves, though their names were difficult to pronounce. After several attempts, she got something on the line of Braek and Selia.

now that she could take a look at them she noticed some details about them;

The man, Braek was broad through the chest, dark-haired, carrying a bow that was clearly handmade. The woman, Selia, instead was shorter, quick in her movements, more graceful. it was her who cooked the _boar? dog? whatever it was_ and with expert hand packed the rest with carefully selected leaves.

Communication was awkward.

Mostly gestures.

A few shared words.

A lot of guessing.

Still, they were kind.

That mattered.

The hunters continued their patrol through the forest and brought Mila along.

At first she found it frustrating.

Then curiosity took over.

The hunters moved through the woods with practiced ease. They tracked animals using signs Mila never would have noticed. Broken twigs. Disturbed leaves. Tiny footprints hidden in the dirt.

More surprising was the magic.

It wasn't dramatic.

No fireballs.

No lightning.

Nothing like the movies.

Sheila occasionally lit up his finger with a small flame or create gusts of wind to distract preys and uncover tracks

Simple tricks.

Yet Mila couldn't stop staring.

The first time she saw the makeshift lighter she nearly walked into a tree.

She laughed so hard he had to sit down.

"Yeah, yeah," Mila muttered. "Very funny."

Over the next day she slowly relaxed.

The hunters shared food.

Showed her edible berries.

Taught her a few words.

And despite the language barrier, Mila found herself weirdly enjoying their company.

Eventually the forest began to thin.

The older hunter pointed ahead.

Mila followed his gaze.

A city stood in the distance.

At first she expected something incredible.

A magical capital filled with floating towers and glowing castles.

Instead it looked surprisingly ordinary.

Wood walls.

Wooden buildings.

A handful of watchtowers.

Smoke rising from chimneys.

People moving through open gates.

It looked like something straight out of a medieval history book.

Small.

Simple.

Normal.

And somehow that made Mila smile.

After days lost in an impossible forest, normal sounded wonderful.

The hunters seemed pleased by her reaction.

Mila smiled back.

She still had no idea where she was.

No idea how to get home.

No idea what waited beyond those walls.

But for the first time since falling through the portal, she found hope.

And that made all the difference.

# Chapter 4 - I'm in prison with a Mailman

The house was suffocating.

Not in the physically—Kael's place had high ceilings and decent ventilation, though it was hard to tell under the mountain of papers—but every wall, every corner, every surface was trying to tell a story Mila didn't want to listen

She'd made it through the first night by pretending none of it was real. A temporary situation. A weird dream that would evaporate once she fell asleep. But morning when morning came, pale sunlight filtering through small windows that looked out onto an unfamiliar street, and she'd woken to the sound of Kael muttering in his office, shuffling documents.

"Uuggh... This is fucking Hell"

The theft, in retrospect, was embarrassingly easy.

Kael had left a small leather pouch on the kitchen table—not even hidden, just _sitting there_, as if trust was something that existed in this world. Mila had watched him move to his desk, distracted by a purple crystal that arrived in a box earlier, and she'd simply... taken it.

A pulse of something electric ran through her. _Actually doing this_.

The pouch felt light, whatever was inside making soft metallic sounds. She'd jammed it into her pocket. And waited for him to notice, to shout, to do something, _Anything_

Kael noticed at dinner.

He was standing over a pot of something that smelled like herbs and boiled vegetables, stirring with one hand while flipping through paperwork with the other, when he patted his hip. Then his desk. Then the table.

He turned around to face her.

Mila straightened her spine, fingers curled into fists in her lap. _Let's see what you do_.

He said something short in his language—the tone was sharp but not shouting—and pointed at her.

"Don't know what you're saying," Mila called back, matching his edge with her own. She lifted her chin. With the _"So what"_ face she spent hours refining back in the principal office at Riverside

He pointed directly at her pocket.

Mila's jaw clenched.

Fine. She was tired of pretending she cared about his stolen property anyway. She pulled out the pouch and tossed it onto the table between them.

Kael caught it one-handed, quickly checked the contents and didn't yell.

Instead, he set it down carefully. He turned off the stove. He said something else, this time slower, each word distinct like he was speaking to a dumb child.

"I. Don't. Understand. you," Mila said, each word equally spaced, equally mocking.

He closed his eyes. Breathed in.

_For someone who's supposed to be my guardian, you're not very good at this_.

When he opened them again, he pulled out that horrible little notebook he'd used yesterday—the one with sketches instead of words—and started drawing. Quick, efficient lines. A small circle. A smaller circle inside. Then what might have been bars around it.

A cage.

He pointed at the drawing, then at her, then at the pouch.

Then he pointed at the door.

Then he drew an arrow pointing away from the circle.

Understanding slid into place with unpleasant weight. "You're saying if I steal from you again, you'll throw me out?"

he must have gotten something from her tone because he nodded once.

"Fine," Mila said, lifting both her hands. "I won't steal from you."

He set the notebook down and went back to the stove, which somehow was even more infuriating than being treated like a baby.

The escape attempt happened three days later.

She'd mapped the house thoroughly by then. The door opened with a keycard Kael kept in his bag—she'd watched him use it multiple times. His bag sat on a shelf in his bedroom when he wasn't actively carrying it. The bedroom door was never locked.

by than she understood subtlety was useless here. Kael noticed everything, eventually. So instead of sneaking—_god, this was stupid_—she just walked in while he was in his office, pulled out the keycard, and headed for the front door.

She made it to the threshold.

Kael's voice behind her was sharp but quiet, the tone of someone stopping a misbehaving dog. He said something that included her name.

Mila didn't turn around. She just tried the door anyway.

Of course the keycard didn't work. Why would it? She was standing there, card in hand, pushing uselessly at a door that wouldn't budge, when Kael reached her.

He didn't take the card or shout or grab her arm. He just... stood there. Waiting. His whole posture screaming he had nowhere else to be, all the time in the world, and infinite patience. It was infuriating.

"I want to leave," Mila said, not bothering to turn. "This is a prison. You're my jailer. This whole situation is insane and I'm not staying—"

He said something short that sounded like a question.

"What?" She finally looked at him.

He gestured to the door, then swept his arm to encompass the whole street outside the small window beside it. Then he folded his arms and raised his eyebrows, waiting.

_Go ahead, then_.

"What do you mean, You said—"

He gestured again, more deliberately. The meaning was clear: the door was his, the street was not.

"So you're letting me leave?"

He stood there fo a second, considering. Then he gestured to her, then to the street, then held up one finger. _One_.

"One what?"

He made a walking motion with two fingers, then pointed outside, then held up his one finger again. Then he pointed back inside the house.

One trip. Outside and back.

"That's insane. I'm not a child—"

But he was already turning away, heading back to his office as if the conversation was finished. As if her threats of escape were a minor inconvenience he'd decided to indulge instead of fight.

Mila stood at the door for another minute before she followed him, seething.

She went outside for exactly three minutes the next morning.

The street was narrow and crowded, early foot traffic heading toward what she assumed was the market district. Everyone was dressed in practical linen and dyed fabrics, and she drew stares immediately—her Earth clothes were too modern, too _obvious_.

She stood on the threshold of Kael's house and watched people pass by, watched them eye her with curiosity or suspicion, the weight of the isolation hit her like a rock.

Three minutes was more than enough.

The fight, at least, felt justified.

It happened on the morning of her sixth day. Kael had been trying to teach her something—actual words, he'd started that the day before, pointing at objects and saying them slowly while she repeated like a particularly stubborn parrot.

Today he'd been trying to teach her numbers, which was somehow even more frustrating than object names. One, two, three—except the sounds weren't even close to the vaguely Germanic phonetics of everything else she'd been learning.

She'd gotten to five and refused to continue.

"This is stupid," she said, standing up from the kitchen table. "I'm never going to learn this. It's incomprehensible. You sound like you're gargling gravel half the time—"

She didn't know which word had done it, but something she'd said made his eye twitch.

He stood up too, and for a moment she thought _finally_, someone was going to react like a normal person. Anger. Offense. Something she could work with.

Instead, he said something calmly, collected his papers, and walked to his office.

"So you just leave? You just _give up_? That's not—UGHH" Mila screamed.

She couldn't hold it in anymore. she wanted to just _Run away_, from this insane situation, from him, from everything

Kael, on the threshold of his office, turned around looking concerned. he stepped towards her to intercept her.

"Leave me ALONE" Mila screamed at him with teary eyes.

It wasn't even a good hit—she'd aimed for his shoulder, to push him away—but he caught her wrist midway through. His grip was careful, not painful, just immobilizing.

"Let me go," she said.

He let go immediately.

She waited for him to retaliate, to grab her harder, to do something else than just _walk away_

instead he just backed off with a stupid apologetic look on his face.

Mila stormed in her _room_—a nook with a sofa some pillows and a paper divider that seemed out of an 40s movie.

That night, Kael left a piece of paper on the kitchen table.

Another list of words. Numbers one through twenty, this time, written out phonetically and with some silly drawings that showed the right mouth shapes for each word.

No note. No explanation.

She stared at it for a long time before she picked it up.

By the time she went to bed, she'd learned to count to ten without sounding like she was trying to dislodge something from her throat.

"I'm getting back home" was the last thing she remembered before she fell into a weird dream where Connor was evil and wanted to destroy the world with a lollipop.

# Chapter 5 - I get my first paycheck
